Medicare Benefit Policy Manual - Chapter 15, Section 220 - Physical Therapy (PT)

Covered Services. Physical therapy services are covered under the Medicare program when they are provided by a qualified physical therapist and are reasonable and necessary for the treatment of a patient's illness or injury. The services must be medically necessary and of a level of complexity that requires the judgment, knowledge, and skills of a qualified physical therapist.

Requirements for Coverage. Services must meet all of the following:

1. The services must be considered safe and effective for the patient's condition.
2. The services must be furnished for the treatment of a specific condition, illness, or injury.
3. The services must be consistent with the diagnosis and documented clinical findings.
4. The services must be necessary and reasonable for the treatment of the condition and must be of a complexity that requires a physical therapist.

Conservative Management. For musculoskeletal conditions such as lumbar radiculopathy, a course of conservative management typically includes a minimum of 4 to 6 weeks of supervised physical therapy, including therapeutic exercise and manual therapy, prior to advanced diagnostic or interventional procedures. The medical record must document the patient's response to therapy, including objective functional improvements.

Maintenance Therapy. Services provided for the general welfare of a patient, such as fitness programs or maintenance therapy that does not require the skills of a physical therapist, are not covered.

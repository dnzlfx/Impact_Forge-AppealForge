LCD L33614 - Occupational Therapy (OT) Services

Indications and Limitations of Coverage. Occupational therapy services are considered medically reasonable and necessary when provided by a qualified occupational therapist for the treatment of a patient's illness or injury to restore function, prevent further disability, or improve the patient's ability to perform activities of daily living (ADLs).

Coverage Criteria. Services must meet ALL of the following:

1. The services must be of a complexity that requires the judgment, knowledge, and skills of a licensed occupational therapist.
2. The services must be furnished under a plan of care established by a physician or the occupational therapist and reviewed by the physician.
3. The services must be reasonable and necessary for the treatment of the specific condition.
4. The services must relate to a documented loss of function or need for training in ADLs, including but not limited to dressing, bathing, feeding, toileting, and instrumental activities of daily living.

Documentation Requirements. The medical record must include the occupational therapy evaluation, the treatment plan with measurable goals, progress notes documenting the patient's response, and the medical necessity for continued services. Services that are maintenance in nature and do not require the skills of an occupational therapist are not covered.

Frequency and Duration. Continued treatment beyond 60 days generally requires re-evaluation and documentation of functional improvement. Plans that demonstrate no objective functional progress over a reasonable trial period may not be considered reasonable and necessary.

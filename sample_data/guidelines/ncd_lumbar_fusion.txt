NCD 150.3 - Lumbar Spinal Fusion

Indications and Limitations of Coverage. Lumbar spinal fusion is considered medically reasonable and necessary only when the following criteria are met:

1. The patient has a documented diagnosis of instability of the lumbar spine, including but not limited to spondylolisthesis, degenerative disc disease with segmental instability, or post-traumatic instability.
2. There is documented failure of an adequate trial of non-operative treatment, generally defined as a minimum of 6 weeks to 3 months of conservative therapy including physical therapy and pain management.
3. There is evidence of progressive neurologic deficit or incapacitating pain that is refractory to conservative measures.
4. The fusion is expected to achieve stabilization and improve the patient's functional status and quality of life.

Documentation Requirements. The medical record must document the history, physical examination findings, imaging studies (including dynamic flexion/extension radiographs where applicable), and the specific indication for fusion. Surgery must be performed for the treatment of the documented condition and not merely for the relief of non-specific low back pain without a definable cause.

Limitations. Fusion performed solely for the treatment of chronic non-specific low back pain in the absence of documented instability or other definitive pathology is not considered medically necessary.

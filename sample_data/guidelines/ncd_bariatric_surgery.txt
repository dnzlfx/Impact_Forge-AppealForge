NCD 100.1 - Bariatric Surgery for Treatment of Morbid Obesity

Indications and Limitations of Coverage. Bariatric surgical procedures are considered medically reasonable and necessary for the treatment of morbid obesity when ALL of the following criteria are met:

1. The patient has a body mass index (BMI) of 40 kg/m2 or greater, OR a BMI of 35 kg/m2 or greater with at least one significant comorbidity that is expected to improve with weight loss, including but not limited to type 2 diabetes mellitus, hypertension, obstructive sleep apnea, or severe osteoarthritis of weight-bearing joints.
2. The patient has been unable to achieve and maintain weight loss despite a documented, supervised, comprehensive weight-loss program of at least 6 months duration.
3. The patient is 18 years of age or older and has been obese for at least 5 years.
4. The patient is mentally and psychologically stable and has no medical contraindications to surgery.
5. The patient demonstrates a commitment to lifelong follow-up, including dietary modification and behavioral change.

Exclusions. Cosmetic weight-loss procedures, repeat procedures without documentation of a new indication, and surgery in patients who do not meet the above criteria are not covered.

Documentation Requirements. The medical record must document the BMI, the duration of obesity, prior weight-loss attempts including duration and modalities, comorbid conditions, psychiatric evaluation when indicated, and the specific procedure planned.

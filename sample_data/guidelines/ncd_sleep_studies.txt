NCD 240.4 - Continuous Positive Airway Pressure (CPAP) Therapy for Obstructive Sleep Apnea

Indications and Limitations of Coverage. Continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP) therapy is considered medically reasonable and necessary for the treatment of obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) when the following criteria are met:

1. A diagnosis of obstructive sleep apnea is established by an attended polysomnography or a home sleep apnea test with an apnea-hypopnea index (AHI) or respiratory disturbance index (RDI) of 15 or more events per hour, OR
2. An AHI or RDI of 5 to 14 events per hour with documented symptoms of excessive daytime sleepiness, impaired cognition, mood disorders, insomnia, hypertension, ischemic heart disease, or history of stroke.

Conditions of Coverage. The patient must be evaluated by a physician specializing in sleep disorders or a physician with specialized training in sleep medicine. An in-office or face-to-face clinical evaluation must be performed prior to the sleep study. The beneficiary must receive education on the proper use of the device and demonstrate improvement in symptoms upon re-evaluation.

Frequency Limitations. Reimbursement for a new CPAP device is not provided within a 5-year period after the initial device, except when significant medical necessity is documented. Replacement supplies (masks, tubing, filters) are covered on a scheduled basis.

Documentation Requirements. The medical record must contain the sleep study report with AHI/RDI, the clinical evaluation, and documentation of symptom improvement after initiation of therapy.

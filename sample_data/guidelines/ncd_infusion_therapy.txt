NCD 190.14 - Home Intravenous Immune Globulin (IVIG) Therapy

Indications and Limitations of Coverage. Home intravenous immune globulin (IVIG) therapy is considered medically reasonable and necessary when ALL of the following criteria are met:

1. The patient has a documented diagnosis for which IVIG therapy is an accepted and medically appropriate treatment, including but not limited to primary immunodeficiency disorders, chronic inflammatory demyelinating polyneuropathy (CIDP), multifocal motor neuropathy, or immune thrombocytopenic purpura.
2. The patient has been stabilized on IVIG therapy in an outpatient or hospital setting and demonstrates an adequate response, OR is clinically stable and capable of being safely managed in the home.
3. The patient or a caregiver is able to participate in the home infusion program and has demonstrated the capacity to manage the infusion, recognize adverse reactions, and respond appropriately.
4. Home environment is appropriate, with access to a telephone and emergency services.

Documentation Requirements. The medical record must document the diagnosis, the specific indication for IVIG, the dosing regimen, the patient's response to therapy, and the plan for monitoring. Adequate supervision by a physician experienced in the management of the underlying condition is required.

Non-covered Indications. Home IVIG for conditions without established evidence of benefit, or when the patient cannot be safely managed at home, is not reasonable and necessary. The service must not duplicate a service provided in another setting.

NCD 150.6 - Total Knee Arthroplasty (TKA)

Indications and Limitations of Coverage. Total knee arthroplasty (TKA) is considered medically reasonable and necessary when ALL of the following criteria are met:

1. The patient has advanced osteoarthritis, rheumatoid arthritis, or other destructive arthropathy of the knee documented by imaging studies (radiographs showing joint space narrowing, subchondral sclerosis, or osteophyte formation).
2. The patient has significant pain and functional limitation that interferes with activities of daily living despite an adequate trial of conservative management, including physical therapy, analgesics, anti-inflammatory medications, and when appropriate, intra-articular injections.
3. The patient is medically cleared for surgery and has no uncontrolled medical conditions that substantially increase surgical risk.
4. The surgery is expected to improve pain, function, and quality of life.

Documentation Requirements. The medical record must document the imaging findings, the severity and duration of symptoms, the trial and results of conservative therapy, the functional limitations, and the medical clearance for surgery.

Non-covered Indications. TKA performed for mild osteoarthritis without functional limitation, for isolated patellofemoral pain without documented arthropathy, or without a documented trial of conservative therapy is not reasonable and necessary.
